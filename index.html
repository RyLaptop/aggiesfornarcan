Free Narcan in A&M dorms and campus buildings

Texas A&M has no written policy on naloxone in public spaces. No rule allows it and no rule bans it, and earlier attempts to put it on campus stalled in that gap. This petition asks the university to write the policy.

What we're asking for

  • A written policy that allows free naloxone (Narcan) in residence halls and public campus buildings.
  • One office that stocks it and replaces it before it expires.
  • Overdose response training for students and staff.

Why

Fake pills made with fentanyl look like Xanax, Percocet, or oxycodone, and students often can't tell the difference. Naloxone is a nasal spray that reverses an opioid overdose within minutes. It is safe to give to someone who turns out not to have taken opioids.

Overdose deaths among 18 to 24 year olds rose 34% between 2018 and 2022, according to CDC data reported by NPR.

Texas State, UT Austin, UTEP, UNT, and UT San Antonio already give students free naloxone. Aggies get none.

Sources: NPR, UT Health San Antonio, KTSM on UTEP, KVUE on UT Austin.

Who is behind this

I'm a Texas A&M student. When I looked into getting Narcan into campus buildings, I found that nothing in university policy says yes or no. People who tried this before told me the gap is where the effort died. I'm collecting signatures from students, parents, and alumni to bring to Risk, Compliance, and Legal.

This is a student effort. Texas A&M University does not sponsor or endorse it.
